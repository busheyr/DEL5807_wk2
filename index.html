1. Introduction

Welcome to Week 2 of DEL 5807: Leadership Foundations! Last week, we examined how leadership theory shifted from fixed traits to learnable skills and behaviors. This week, we explore Authentic Leadership—a framework that addresses the core of executive identity, personal character, and ethical transparency.

In an era marked by rapid technological disruption, societal uncertainty, and organizational crises, followers do not simply demand strategic execution; they demand bona fide leaders they can trust. This module examines both the practical and theoretical models of authentic leadership, helping you articulate your 'True North' and leverage your critical life experiences to guide your executive practice.

2. Module Key Takeaways

  1. 1Authenticity is a Lifelong Process, Not a Fixed Trait: Authentic leadership is developed over a lifetime and is continually shaped by critical life events and personal reflection.
  2. 2The Dual Frameworks of Authenticity: Practical models (Bill George) focus on essential characteristics (e.g., Purpose, Values, Heart), while theoretical models (Walumbwa et al.) define four core self-regulatory behaviors (Self-Awareness, Internalized Moral Perspective, Balanced Processing, Relational Transparency).
  3. 3Trigger Events as Catalysts: Critical life experiences—both positive achievements and severe crucibles—act as catalysts that trigger growth, deepen self-knowledge, and define your executive stance.

3. Section 1: The Four Perspectives of Authentic Leadership

Intrapersonal Perspective:

Focuses on what occurs within the leader. Emphasizes self-knowledge, self-regulation, and the subjective meaning leaders attach to their life experiences.

Interpersonal Perspective:

Views authenticity as a reciprocal process created jointly by leaders and followers. Authenticity emerges through mutual trust, transparency, and shared relationships.

Developmental Perspective:

Views authentic leadership as a capability that can be nurtured over time rather than an innate property. It is grounded in positive psychological capacities and ethics.

Behavioral Perspective:

Defines authenticity through four specific, observable behaviors: Self-Awareness, Internalized Moral Perspective, Balanced Processing, and Relational Transparency.

4. Section 2: Bill George’s Practical Approach (Characteristics & Dimensions)

Dimension 1: Purpose & Passion

Core Framework: Authentic leaders know what they stand for and where they are going. They possess deep-seated intrinsic motivation and genuine passion for their work.

Executive Application

Executive Application: Passion drives long-term strategic persistence during organizational turbulence.

Dimension 2: Values & Behavior ('True North')

Core Framework: Leaders understand their core ethical principles and act in alignment with them. When tested under high-stakes pressures, authentic leaders do not compromise their values; they use challenges to strengthen them.

Executive Application

Executive Application: Establishing clear values prevents ethical derailment and builds governance credibility.

Dimension 3: Relationships & Connectedness

Core Framework: Leaders open themselves up to establish genuine trust with stakeholders. They share their stories and listen to others, creating high-quality, transparent connections.

Executive Application

Executive Application: Softening rigid hierarchical boundaries fosters higher team loyalty, engagement, and productivity.

Dimension 4: Self-Discipline & Consistency

Core Framework: Self-discipline gives executives focus, determination, and operational consistency. Under stress, disciplined leaders remain calm and predictable.

Executive Application

Executive Application: Predictable, value-aligned executive behavior gives teams a sense of security and clarity during organizational change.

Dimension 5: Heart & Compassion

Core Framework: Sensitivity to the needs of others and a willingness to understand diverse life stories and backgrounds.

Executive Application

Executive Application: Cultivating empathy enhances cultural awareness, inclusion, and organizational psychological safety.

5. Section 3: The Theoretical Model of Authentic Leadership

1. Self-Awareness:

Deeply understanding one's own strengths, weaknesses, values, emotions, and impact on others. It is an ongoing process of reflection rather than a static destination.

2. Internalized Moral Perspective:

Guiding decisions and behaviors using internal ethical standards rather than bowing to external groupthink, toxic pressures, or short-term demands.

3. Balanced Processing:

Objectively analyzing all relevant data and soliciting opposing viewpoints before making a final executive decision. It requires remaining unbiased and avoiding favoritism.

4. Relational Transparency:

Being genuine, open, and real in relationships by appropriately sharing core feelings, motives, and constructive vulnerabilities.

6. Section 4: Antecedents & Catalysts of Executive Growth

1. Positive Psychological Capacities (PsyCap)

Confidence (Self-Efficacy), Hope, Optimism, and Resilience (bouncing back from severe adversity).

2. Moral Reasoning

The capacity to make ethical decisions that transcend individual self-interest and align stakeholders toward the greater common good.

3. Critical Life Events (Crucibles)

Major life events—whether positive or negative—serve as primary trigger events. The meaning you assign to your crucible moments forms the foundation of your authentic story and executive identity.
